The 4-Phase Himalayan Flash Flood Cycle (Why It Is NOT Just Water)

In steep mountain terrain, flash floods are hyper-dense debris flows rather than slow flat-land inundation:

PHASE 1

🌧️ Saturation Trigger

Cloudburst rainfall (>50 mm/h) saturates soil moisture past 85–90%. Pore-water pressure spikes and soil friction vanishes.

PHASE 2

⛰️ Slope Failure

Factor of Safety drops below 1.0. Thousands of tons of soil, boulders, and trees collapse into mountain streams.

PHASE 3

🚧 Temporary Damming

Landslide mass chokes narrow V-shaped gorge. Millions of liters of water accumulate rapidly behind the debris dam.

PHASE 4

🌊 Catastrophic Surge

Dam breaches under hydraulic pressure. A 20–40 km/h hyper-dense slurry ($1.8\text{--}2.2\text{ g/cm}^3$) rushes downstream!

🔬 Geomorphic Kinetic Impact Equation:
E_k = ½ · m · v² | Density: ρ_debris ≈ 2.0 g/cm³ (2x heavier than water, destroying bridges & roads)
